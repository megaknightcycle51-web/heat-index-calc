Heat Index Calculator

What is the Heat Index?
The Heat Index (also called the “apparent temperature”) combines air temperature and relative humidity to describe what the temperature actually feels like to the human body. High humidity makes it harder for sweat to evaporate—so, on humid days, you feel hotter than the thermometer shows.

How is the Heat Index calculated?
For temperatures below 20°C, humidity has little effect on how warm it feels. Only once the temperature reaches 27°C or higher does humidity start to meaningfully increase the perceived heat. The Heat Index is calculated using a complex formula that takes into account both the temperature and relative humidity (minimum 40%).
The resulting Heat Index is compared to a warning table, which makes it easy to understand the risk of heat-related illness at a glance.

How to use:
Enter a temperature (in °C) and relative humidity (as a percentage). Press "Calculate Heat Index" to see how it actually feels—and whether you should take precautions.

Warning table:

  • Safe: < 27°C — No heat stress expected.
  • Caution: 27–32°C — Possible heat cramps or exhaustion.
  • Extreme Caution: 32–41°C — Likely heat cramps/exhaustion.
  • Danger: 41–54°C — High risk: heat stroke possible.
  • Extreme Danger: >= 54°C — Heat stroke imminent without protection!
